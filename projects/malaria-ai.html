ACADEMIC & ENGINEERING PROJECT

Comparative Analysis of Malaria Detection Techniques using AI

Sep 2021 – Apr 2022

Associated with MKSSS Cummins College of Engineering for Women

Project description

My B.Tech Final Year Project was on the topic of “Comparative Analysis of Malaria Detection Techniques using AI”, with the focus on making a comparative analysis of malaria detection techniques using three models of Deep Learning, namely: CNN, Resnet 50, and VGG 19.

1. I started from scratch by delving into understanding traditional detection techniques and finding loopholes in the techniques, thereby coming up with a more accurate and faster detection technique.

2. To build on the research for this project, I engaged in good literature survey studies covering malaria cell image classification using Image processing, Machine Learning, and Convolutional Neural Networks.

3. I worked on a smaller dataset from Kaggle for training on varied models and implementing those for bigger datasets from official health websites for testing purposes. I proactively worked with varied parameters to find the most accurate and precise model for malaria detection.

4. Furthermore, I was involved with my team in displaying the output on a User Interface of an app designed by the team.

5. The initial challenge for the team was to come up with a model that performs well on parameters like sensitivity and specificity. My team and I came up with some novel solutions and tested various models to come up with the best model, that’s how we came up with the idea of using the Ensemble CNN Model for this problem statement.

6. This was notable as the model succeeded in achieving the desired results, giving maximum accuracy among all models and performing relatively better on other parameters as well.

7. The model outperformed the other two models. VGG-19, ResNet-50, and CNN Ensemble Models were implemented and compared on main parameters: Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and F1-Score, wherein the CNN Ensembled Model outperformed other models and achieved maximum accuracy of 95.22%.
