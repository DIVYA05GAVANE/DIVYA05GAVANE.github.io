ACADEMIC & ENGINEERING PROJECT

Literature Review: Random Lasers – Physics, Materials & Emerging Applications

Oct 2025 – Nov 2025

Associated with National University of Singapore

Project description

Conducted an in-depth literature review on random lasers, focusing on their fundamental operating principles, historical evolution, and recent advances in disordered photonic systems. Analyzed mirrorless lasing mechanisms based on multiple scattering and optical gain, including Letokhov’s diffusion model, threshold conditions, and coherence regimes.

Performed a comparative evaluation of random laser implementations across material systems such as ZnO and GaN semiconductor nanostructures, dye–nanoparticle composites, polymer-based systems, perovskite hybrids, and biological media. Assessed performance trade-offs in terms of lasing threshold, coherence, stability, directionality, and material reliability, linking optical behavior to gain–scattering interplay and device structure.

Reviewed modern trends including plasmonic-assisted random lasers, hybrid perovskite systems, flexible and wearable photonic devices, biosensing, and neuromorphic photonics. Identified key limitations related to spectral stability, efficiency, scalability, and reproducibility, and highlighted open research challenges for practical integration of random lasers in next-generation photonic and optoelectronic applications.
